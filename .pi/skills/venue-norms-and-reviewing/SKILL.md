# Venue norms and reviewing

Two audiences use this skill: the supervisor (grading a PR or a manuscript)
and the author choosing where to submit. Both use the same vocabulary, so the
grade in `review_write` and the grade in an audit cycle mean the same thing.

## Grade scale (7 points + journal mapping)

| Grade | Journal equivalent | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `strong reject` | reject | fatal: results unsupported or unreproducible |
| `reject` | reject | major defects, not fixable in one revision |
| `weak reject` | reject | claim-affecting defects; a fix list exists |
| `borderline` | major revision | defensible but unresolved claim-affecting issues |
| `weak accept` | minor revision | correct, evidence mostly complete, clear fix list |
| `accept` | accept | no confirmed S0/S1, gates green, claims traceable |
| `strong accept` | accept | above, plus a significant, well-evidenced advance |

**Blocking rules** (from `research_agent/plan/07_AUDIT_HARNESS.md` §7):

- any **confirmed S0**, or a confirmed S1 in methodology/statistics/UQ/claims
  → cap at `weak reject`;
- an unfixed confirmed S1 elsewhere → cap at `borderline`;
- a failed deterministic gate → cap at `weak reject` unless the adjudicator
  records an inapplicability reason;
- `accept`/`strong accept` require no confirmed S0/S1, all gates green,
  claims↔artifact traceability complete, and the limitation disclosures
  present;
- an **uncalibrated** audit (historical recall < 0.6) may not grade the
  paper at all.

Grades come with a **fix list**, each item tagged feasible/needs-human with
an owner and an effort estimate. A grade without a fix list is not a review.

## Reviewer criteria (score each, with an evidence pointer)

1. **Novelty/contribution** — compare against the closest prior work, not
   against a strawman (see `literature-review-and-verification`).
2. **Correctness** — formulas vs code, estimand vs claim, tests that would
   catch the defect.
3. **Evidence/experiments** — baselines actually run, frozen configs, no
   test-horizon selection, multiple-comparison disclosure.
4. **Reproducibility** — seeds, `target_accept`, manifests, data hashes,
   environment fingerprint, one-command reproduction.
5. **Clarity** — notation consistency, table/figure honesty, limitations.
6. **Significance** — would a practitioner change practice?

Every score carries the command or file:line that produced it. "Looks fine"
is not evidence; "re-ran `pytest tests/test_regularization.py`, 42 passed at
head sha X" is.

## Venues

Read `references/venues.md` for the four target venues and their expected
framing. Quick orientation:

- **Journal of Forecasting** — applied forecasting; wants an honest
  evaluation protocol and practical value; the project default
  (`.pi/config/project.json`).
- **International Journal of Forecasting** — broader forecasting science;
  strict about baselines, horizon framing and statistical comparison.
- **Machine Learning** — methodological novelty, theory and identifiability;
  weak on "a package exists" as a contribution.
- **Journal of Statistical Software** — software paper; needs a stable API,
  documented examples, reproducible installation, comparison to alternatives.

## Desk-reject triggers (check before submitting)

- Numbers in the text that no artifact backs.
- A headline built on test-set selection (grid/best-of-N on the test
  horizon) or on baselines that are not what they are called.
- "Bit-for-bit reproducibility" claims without seeds/hashes/pins.
- LaTeX that does not build, missing bibliography entries, or citations to
  nonexistent works.
- Missing limitations/AI-use/data-availability statements.
- Results that cannot be regenerated by the named commands in the paper.

## Review forms

Use `templates/review_form.md` for supervisor reviews and
`templates/review_form.md` (the same structure) for audit synthesis. Author ≠
reviewer: if you wrote the diff, you cannot review it.

## Failure modes

- Grading by vibes: a grade without gate results and a fix list is not
  admissible; the conductor rejects it.
- "Minor revision" for a confirmed S1 → violates the blocking rule; the cap
  is `weak reject`/`borderline`.
- Reviewing against a different head SHA than the PR record → the review does
  not count; re-check `head_sha`.
- Choosing JSS for a methods result (or ML for a package) → mismatched
  contribution type; reread `references/venues.md` before writing the cover
  letter.
